## 1. Context

PJ OWL makes its source code available to the public and aims to provide free access to individuals, freelancers, MEIs, and small businesses.

The project may develop commercial offerings in the future. The licensing model must therefore distinguish between using the application in a user's own commercial activity and commercially exploiting the application itself.

## 2. Decision

Adopt the custom **PJ OWL Free Use and Restricted Commercial Distribution License**, subject to legal review and confirmation of the rights held by the project owner and contributors.

The chosen permissions are:

- Free personal, educational, professional, and business use.
- Permission to study and modify the source code.
- Permission to redistribute the original version free of charge.
- Permission to redistribute modified versions free of charge, provided that attribution, license, modification notices, and corresponding source code are preserved.
- Prohibition on third-party commercial exploitation of the Software and derivative versions, including resale, paid access, paid hosting, paid subscriptions, white-label commercial editions, and monetized distribution.

## 3. Important Distinction

Users may earn money from their own products and services while using PJ OWL.

The restriction concerns monetizing PJ OWL itself, not the user's business activity.

For example, a business may use PJ OWL to issue receipts to paying customers, but may not sell access to the application.

## 4. Project Classification

Because this license restricts commercial exploitation by third parties, PJ OWL should be described as **source-available with restricted commercial distribution**, not as Open Source under the Open Source Definition.

## 5. Ownership and Contributor Rights

AllebStrix is identified as the project owner, maintainer, and licensing contact.

The ownership of each contribution and the authority to license it must nevertheless be supported by applicable agreements and law.

Before claiming exclusive ownership of the entire project or licensing rights belonging to other contributors, the necessary written assignments, permissions, or agreements must be obtained.

## 6. Future Review

The licensing decision should be reviewed if the project changes its distribution model, introduces paid services, accepts substantial external contributions, or changes ownership.
